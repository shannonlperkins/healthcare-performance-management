# Design Methodology

## Objective

Create a performance-management system that gives employees clearer expectations and gives leaders a more consistent, evidence-based method for evaluating performance.

## Design Principle 1: Define observable behavior

Each rating level should describe what a leader can actually observe rather than relying on labels such as “good attitude” or “team player.”

A five-level scale allows leaders to distinguish between:

- performance requiring improvement;
- developing consistency;
- reliable expected performance;
- performance that regularly exceeds expectations;
- exceptional impact that improves the broader team or system.

## Design Principle 2: Combine behaviors and results

A strong employee performance framework should not evaluate only output metrics or only soft skills.

This model combines:

- values and behaviors;
- communication and teamwork;
- operational expectations;
- role-specific quality or accuracy measures.

This helps prevent a single metric from becoming the entire performance conversation.

## Design Principle 3: Require evidence

Ratings above or below expected performance should be supported by examples, measurable outcomes, documented observations, or relevant operational data.

Evidence improves clarity during calibration and helps employees understand why a rating was assigned.

## Design Principle 4: Use role-specific measures carefully

Operational measures should be relevant to the employee's work and reasonably within that employee's ability to influence.

The portfolio workbook includes sanitized examples for:

- patient access;
- clinical support;
- nurse triage;
- imaging.

These are illustrative rather than employer-specific thresholds.

## Design Principle 5: Add calibration

The framework supports two evaluators so leaders can compare ratings and discuss meaningful differences before a review is finalized.

The objective is not to force identical ratings. It is to identify where expectations or evidence may be interpreted differently.

## Design Principle 6: Separate scoring from HR policy

The original professional work included policy-driven performance logic. In this public portfolio version, formal corrective-action status triggers a **management review flag** rather than automatically imposing a final rating.

This intentionally separates the analytical framework from organization-specific HR policy.

## Design Principle 7: End with development

The review concludes with forward-looking questions about:

- what energizes the employee;
- what meaningful goal they want to pursue;
- what support leadership can provide.

This turns the performance review into both an accountability system and a development conversation.

## Implementation Approach

1. Define competencies and role expectations.
2. Establish rating anchors and evidence standards.
3. Identify appropriate operational data sources.
4. Train and calibrate evaluators.
5. Pilot the framework with a limited employee group.
6. Review rating distributions and ambiguous criteria.
7. Refine the framework before broader use.
8. Reassess metrics annually as operational priorities change.

## Portfolio Confidentiality Note

This methodology is based on professional work I completed in healthcare operations. Employer names, employee information, internal performance results, exact benchmarks, proprietary links, and organization-specific policies have been removed or generalized.
